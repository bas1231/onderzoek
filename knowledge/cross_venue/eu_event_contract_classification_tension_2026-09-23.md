# EU event-contract classification tension — 2026-09-23

Status: `FACT_VERIFIED` for ESMA rule position; `WATCH` for Polymarket regulatory strategy; economic status `NO_PROVEN_EDGE`.

## Observation

ESMA's 3 July 2026 primary statement says event contracts must be assessed by substance and underlying. Where an event contract is a financial instrument, ESMA says it is a derivative and, because of the binary outcome, falls within national product-intervention measures prohibiting marketing, distribution or sale of binary options to retail clients. Distribution of qualifying event contracts in the EU also requires investment-firm authorisation even for non-retail clients.

On 23 September 2026 the Financial Times reported that Polymarket is lobbying European/UK authorities to have prediction-market contracts treated as financial products rather than gambling products, including engagement with ESMA, the European Commission and the FCA. This is reputable-secondary evidence for Polymarket's current regulatory strategy; no primary Polymarket statement was found in the scheduled sweep.

## Mechanism / specialist interpretation

There is a non-trivial classification tension: obtaining a financial-product classification does not imply EU retail access. For event contracts that fall within MiFID financial-instrument scope, ESMA's existing position points toward derivative/binary-option treatment and existing national retail product-intervention prohibitions. Therefore `financial product classification -> broader EU retail availability` is an unsafe inference.

This is an access/product-lifecycle WATCH trigger, not a trading candidate. A future venue/product change could alter eligible participants, product mix, liquidity segmentation, geofencing, fee structure or route/venue architecture, but none of those economic effects is established now.

## Falsification / guardrails

- Do not infer that all event contracts are financial instruments; ESMA explicitly says classification depends on the event question.
- Do not infer that all event contracts are gambling products either.
- Do not infer that lobbying succeeds or that any regulator has changed its rule position.
- Do not infer executable edge from regulatory fragmentation.
- No strategy build, live trading, paid action, wallet action or restricted access is warranted.
